Y is a connection game (Schensted–Titus, 1953) played on a triangular board of
side *n*: *n* rows, *n*(*n*+1)/2 cells. Each move places one stone, and you win
by connecting all three sides of the triangle with a single group. A filled
board always has exactly one winner, so Y can never be drawn, and a
strategy-stealing argument shows the first player wins at every size. Hex
reduces to Y.

Sides 1 through 6 were strongly solved in 2026 by Brian Liou's game-solver, a
retrograde solve that computes the value of every position. Three
independent solve paths agree on every value. The explorer below plays perfect moves for sides 3–5, solving each board in your browser.
[Sources and prior art](/games/connection/y/).

All six sizes are strongly solved.

*† Sides 1–5 count reachable positions; side 6 is the dense-index space
(reachable positions were not separately enumerated). The explorer solves over
the dense index, so its side-5 count (3,492,117) is slightly higher than the
reachable count shown here.*

## Open: side 7

Side 7 (28 cells) is unsolved. Its dense index exceeds 2³² (an estimated
10¹²–10¹³ positions), so a solve needs 64-bit indexing and a value table larger
than RAM. See the [open problem](/games/connection/y/#open-y-side-7).
